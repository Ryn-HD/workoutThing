# Exercise Cues (Ryan's Setup)

Per-exercise setup and execution cues, captured as we work through gear/technique
problems. These are meant to travel with the Liftoscript program (attached as
exercise notes) once the program is built. See [equipment.md](equipment.md) for the
gear these assume and [exercise-selection-and-sfr.md](exercise-selection-and-sfr.md)
for why each exercise was picked.

## Rear Delt: Standing Single-Arm Cable Reverse Fly (program default)

Program uses this **standing** version — no bench, no lying down, **no foam block**.
The prone/face-down DB versions all fight the wide SelectTechs (DBs hit the floor at
stretch unless the body is raised on a block — uncomfortable), so they're avoided.

- Stand **side-on** to the cable set at ~shoulder height, D-handle in the outside hand
  reaching **across** your body.
- Soft fixed elbow bend. Pull the handle **across and out** in an arc away from the
  midline (like the second half of a "spread"), leading with the elbow, until your arm
  is out to the side. Squeeze the rear delt, control back to a stretch across the body.
- Cue: **"spread," not "row."** Strict, 12-20 reps. One arm at a time (cable is single).

Block-free DB alternative if you ever want to free the cable: **prone incline reverse
fly** — bench at ~30°, lie face-DOWN, arms hang off the *sides* of the pad (past the
edges, so wide DBs clear), spread out to a T. Not the default, but a legal fallback.

## Back (horizontal): Single-Arm DB Row (replaces chest-supported row)

The chest-supported DB row was dropped — on a low bench the wide SelectTechs hit the
floor at full stretch and need a foam block under you (uncomfortable). This clears
that with no block.

- One **knee and one hand on the bench**, torso roughly horizontal, other foot on the
  floor for a base. Free DB hangs straight down **beside the bench** into open space —
  because your torso is up at bench height and the DB is off to the side, it clears the
  floor at full stretch, no block.
- Let the shoulder **protract/reach down** at the bottom for a lat stretch, then row
  the DB toward your hip (not your armpit), driving the elbow back and down. Keep the
  torso still — don't twist to heave it up.
- Strict, ~10 reps/arm.

## Lower Back: Back Extension (KOT-style, home versions)

For spinal-erector resilience ("build the lower back"). Programmed as a **Lower A
finisher only** (squat day) — never on deadlift day, to avoid stacking erector fatigue.

- **Floor/mats version (start here):** rolled yoga mats under the hips as a fulcrum,
  **feet braced against a wall** (or under a loaded barbell), extend the torso up a few
  inches from a slight forward fold. Limited ROM but a fine starting point.
- **Bench version (fuller ROM upgrade):** hips on the edge of the flat bench, torso
  hanging off the front, **heels anchored** under the rack's low safety pins or a
  loaded barbell on the floor. Fold down, then extend up to a **straight line — do not
  hyperextend past straight.** Hands at chest (easier) or behind head (harder).
- Cue: for lower-back building keep a slight arch and **extend with the erectors**;
  don't round-and-drive with the hips (that turns it into a glute/ham move). Controlled,
  no jerking, 12-15 reps.

## Lat Pullover vs Lat Pulldown — pick one, they're different exercises

The "kneeling lat prayer" (curving the bar toward the waist, straightening up
mid-rep) was accidentally blending these two movements. Retire that version — use
one of the following depending on what the slot is for.

**DB Pullover** (lat isolation + stretch — replaces the kneeling cable version):
- Lie back on the bench, hold one DB cupped in both hands over your chest, soft
  fixed elbow bend (don't bend/press with the elbows during the rep).
- Lower it in an arc **back behind your head** until you feel a deep lat/rib
  stretch.
- Pull it back over your chest by driving with your **lats/armpits** (imagine
  pulling your upper arms toward your hips). Keep ribs down, don't overarch the
  low back.

**Lat Pulldown** (vertical pull for width):
- Half-kneel or sit, set a **fixed ~20-30° lean-back and hold it** — don't
  straighten up mid-rep.
- Initiate by pulling your **shoulder blades down** first, then drive elbows down
  toward your hip pockets.
- Pull the bar to your **upper chest/collarbone — not your waist**. Bar-to-waist
  turns it into the curved straight-arm hybrid and loses the vertical lat line.
- Let the bar rise to **full arm extension** at the top for the stretch.

## Face Pulls — use the LOW pulley, not the high anchor

The rack's high anchor point is too steep for a standard face pull angle. Move the
cable to the **low (row) position** instead, sit on the floor or a low seat facing
it with the rope attached, and pull toward your **forehead with elbows high and
flaring back**, rotating knuckles up at the end of the rep. Optional/nice-to-have
once the prone incline fly is covering rear delt volume — not required.
