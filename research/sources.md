# Sources

## Stronger by Science / Greg Nuckols

- [The New Approach to Training Volume](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/the-new-approach-to-training-volume/) - argues that, for hypertrophy, weekly hard sets per muscle are generally more useful than `sets x reps x load`.
- [The "Hypertrophy Rep Range" - Fact or Fiction?](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/hypertrophy-range-fact-fiction/) - summarizes evidence that hypertrophy can occur across broad rep ranges when sets are close to failure.
- [Training Frequency for Muscle Growth: What the Data Say](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/frequency-muscle/) - frequency matters less when volume is equated, but higher frequency can help distribute volume productively.
- [Are supersets THE most underrated workout hack?](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/supersets-research/) - supports agonist-antagonist supersets as a time-saving strategy with similar hypertrophy outcomes in the discussed study; cautions around high volumes and very taxing technical lifts.
- [The Evidence is Lacking for "Effective Reps"](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/effective-reps/) - cautions against over-precise effective-rep counting; hard sets per muscle are useful but imperfect.
- [Do Lengthened Partials Really Stimulate "Stretch-Mediated Hypertrophy"?](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/stretch-mediated-hypertrophy/) - the growth edge for lengthened partials over full ROM is real but small (~+6.76%); argues most such studies are NOT actually showing the stretch-mediated mechanism the term implies.
- [Periodization: What the Data Say](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/periodization-data/) - the model of periodization matters less than ensuring progression and fatigue management; periodization helps strength more clearly than hypertrophy.
- [Periodization: History and Theory](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/periodization-history-theory/) - background on periodization models.
- [Daily Undulating Periodization](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/daily-undulating-periodization/) - DUP works but isn't magic; it's one valid way to distribute rep ranges/intensity.
- [The Science of Autoregulation](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/autoregulation/) - rationale and methods for adjusting load/volume to daily readiness.
- [Training Frequency for Strength: What the Data Say](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/training-frequency/) - strength benefits a bit more from higher frequency under volume-matched conditions.

## 3DMJ / Eric Helms

- [Rest-Pause/Myo Reps and Long Rest Periods](https://www.3dmusclejourney.com/blog/rest-pause-myo-reps-and-long-rest-periods) - practical takeaways: use short-rest/rest-pause/myo-rep methods mainly on non-tiring isolation exercises; use antagonist paired sets to save time; avoid excessive cardiometabolic fatigue on compounds.
- [How Much of Your Volume is Actually Effective?](https://www.3dmusclejourney.com/blog/how-much-of-your-volume-is-actually-effective) - emphasizes rep quality, proximity to failure, and whether volume is actually productive.
- [Hypertrophy Programming for Bodybuilders](https://www.3dmusclejourney.com/hypertrophy) - course page, useful mainly as confirmation of 3DMJ's volume/intensity/frequency framework.
- [Muscle Group Specialization Cycles (Part 2)](https://3dmusclejourney.com/muscle-group-specialization-cycles-part-2/) - how to push a lagging muscle's volume up while holding others at maintenance.
- [Autoregulation Training Talk with Eric Helms](https://dynamicduotraining.com/autoregulation-training-talk-with-eric-helms/) - Helms on RPE/RIR autoregulation and setting up an autoregulated mesocycle ("embedding a respect for individual variation").

## Menno Henselmans

- [New science on the optimal training volume: extreme training for extreme gains?](https://mennohenselmans.com/optimal-training-volume/) - argues that common 10-15 set recommendations may be conservative for some people and reviews higher-volume findings.
- [Is there a maximum productive training volume per session?](https://mennohenselmans.com/maximum-productive-training-volume-per-session/) - key article: productive volume is ~9-13 sets per muscle per session; reconciles the conflicting volume studies (the "high volume is bad" studies were really "too much per session, too low frequency").

## Renaissance Periodization / Mike Israetel

- [Training Volume Landmarks for Muscle Growth](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/training-volume-landmarks-muscle-growth) - the MV / MEV / MAV / MRV framework for per-muscle weekly volume and how to progress within a mesocycle.
- SFR (stimulus-to-fatigue ratio) is also an Israetel/RP concept; see exercise-selection note.

- [Jeff Nippard ranks triceps exercises (BarBend)](https://barbend.com/news/jeff-nippard-ranks-best-and-worst-triceps-exercises/) - overhead cable ext best (long-head stretch, ~40% > pushdowns per a 2023 study).
- [Milo Wolf - best exercises for long-length partials (BarBend)](https://barbend.com/best-exercises-for-long-length-partial-reps/) - lateral raises, curls, pushdowns, most back work, flyes; ~50% reps as partials, ~5-10% edge.
- [Menno Henselmans - best exercises to build muscle II](https://mennohenselmans.com/these-are-the-best-exercises-to-build-muscle-science-based-ii/) - biceps "holy trinity" (barbell/preacher/incline curl); hip thrust ≈ squat for glutes; incline ≈ flat bench.
- [Menno Henselmans - 7 dazzling new studies 2025](https://mennohenselmans.com/7-dazzling-new-studies-for-serious-lifters-2025/) - **2025: lengthened-bias ≈ shortened over 10 weeks; full ROM matters more than peak-stretch tension.** Supersets re-confirmed as time-savers with no hypertrophy penalty.

## Primary Research And Reviews

- [Schoenfeld, Ogborn, Krieger 2017 - weekly volume meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27433992/) - dose-response relationship between weekly resistance training volume and muscle mass.
- [Schoenfeld, Grgic, Krieger 2019 - frequency meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30558493/) - examines how many times per week a muscle should be trained for hypertrophy.
- [Schoenfeld et al. 2016 - longer rest periods](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26605807/) - longer interset rests improved strength and hypertrophy in resistance-trained men in this study.
- [Pallares et al. 2021 - range of motion meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34170576/) - systematic review/meta-analysis on range of motion and resistance-training adaptations.
- [Effects of range of motion on muscle development during resistance training interventions 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32030125/) - systematic review of full vs partial ROM training and muscle development.
- [Effects of Drop Sets on Skeletal Muscle Hypertrophy 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37523092/) - systematic review/meta-analysis on drop sets and hypertrophy.
- [Acute and Chronic Effects of Drop-Set Training 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41920484/) - newer systematic review/meta-analysis on drop-set training.
- [Lengthened partials vs full ROM in trained individuals (PeerJ 2025)](https://peerj.com/articles/18904/) - lengthened partials elicited similar (slightly favorable) adaptations vs full ROM.
- Higher-weekly-volume studies cited by Menno (trained each muscle 3x/week, so ~10 sets/session): Radaelli et al. 2015 ([PubMed](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/25546444)) and Schoenfeld et al. 2019 ([PubMed](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/30153194)) found more growth at 45 vs 30 vs 15 sets/muscle/week.
- "Too much volume" studies that were really low-frequency: Barbalho et al. 2018/2019, Amirthalingam et al. 2017 (GVT, [PubMed](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/27941492)), Heaselgrave et al. 2019 ([PubMed](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/30160627)) - all trained the muscle 1-2x/week, so their high-volume groups exceeded the per-session ceiling.
